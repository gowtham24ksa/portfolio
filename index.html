Sambalam, 2026, designed and shipped solo with an AI coding agent

A Tamil wage app that replaced a contractor's notebook

Problem
A contractor in Tamil Nadu paid ₹2L+ a week in cash from a paper notebook. One slip and a crew got paid wrong.
Insight
He asked for expense totals. He needed the balance left after advances. His margins were full of those sums.
Decision
No typing for daily entry. Weekly settlement that deducts advances itself. Per-crew locking. Tamil by default, works offline.
Result
Half the sites used it at first. I sat with the ones who had stopped, fixed what they pointed at. Now all of them run on it.

Ledgerline, 2026, built on Claude's MCP

Investor updates written in Claude, published without it

Founders write their monthly update in their own Claude. Ledgerline stores it as data, hosts it as a page, emails each investor a personal link and shows who opened it. Investors never need Claude.

Problem
Updates get written from scratch, contradict last month, and vanish into inboxes with no idea who read them.
Decision
No AI inside the product. Five MCP tools hand Claude six periods of history, take a draft back, publish it. Runway, burn multiple, NRR: plain arithmetic, cited thresholds.
Guardrails
Drafts get checked: runway that disagrees with cash ÷ burn, a metric that moved 50%+ in a month, missing numbers. Published periods can't be overwritten.

How I work

The same three habits, whether the user is a Citibank relationship manager or a mason in Mettupalayam.

Find the problem upstream of the one you were given

The CRM brief was "automate workflows". The real leak was spreadsheets feeding it bad data. The contractor asked for expense totals and needed balances after advances.

Make the case in the stakeholder's own numbers

Revenue for Citibank, working capital for the plant, hours saved for the contractor. If the case doesn't hold in the budget meeting, the product doesn't ship.

Ship it, then go back and watch people use it

Adoption is the number that tells the truth. Both times it stalled, the fix came from watching people use the thing, not from adding features.
